Rapportive is gone. The idea still works.

SenderFinder brings back the simple part of Rapportive: open an email in Gmail and see who sent it. Open source, your own API keys, no account.

Coming to the Chrome Web Store How it works

What Rapportive was

Rapportive was a Gmail add-on that showed the LinkedIn profile of the person you were emailing, next to the message. LinkedIn acquired it in 2012 and later shut it down.

What SenderFinder does

When you open an email in Gmail, the browser's side panel shows the sender's name, headline, company, location and a link to their LinkedIn profile. Other people in the thread (other senders, To, Cc) are listed below and looked up only when you click them. The email itself only gets a small "in" mark next to the sender.

Outlook on the web is supported on a best-effort basis.

What is different

  • Open source. AGPL-3.0 licensed, with the code on GitHub. No company can switch it off for you.
  • Your own keys. Profiles come from data providers you choose: Monid or treg, with Firecrawl as an optional fallback. The extension calls them directly from your browser.
  • No account. No signup, no server in the lookup path. The only thing sent to us is a daily anonymous usage count, which you can switch off.
  • No CRM lock-in. History stays in your browser. Export it as JSON or CSV whenever you want.
  • A name check. If the provider's name doesn't match the display name in the email, the result is marked "uncertain" and shows both names.

Honest limitations

  • You need a provider key. One key from Monid or treg is enough to start.
  • Lookups cost a little. A successful lookup typically costs between $0.0005 and $0.03, usually well under a cent. Misses on most providers are free, and results are cached so an address is never paid for twice.
  • Spending has limits you set. A per-lookup cap (default $0.05) and a daily cap (default $2) apply. Only the sender of the opened email is looked up automatically.
  • No Firefox yet. It runs in Chrome. Desktop mail apps are not supported.
  • Coverage varies. Not everyone will be found, and personal addresses such as gmail.com are skipped by default. A button lets you look them up anyway.

Get started

  1. Install the extension. Until the Chrome Web Store listing is live, install it from source.
  2. Create an API key at Monid or treg.
  3. Paste it into the extension's settings and open an email in Gmail.
